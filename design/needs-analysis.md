# Needs analysis

**Client (fictional):** Northwind Logistics, a regional distribution center with 140 staff. Near-miss reports are up this quarter. The company and every detail below are fictional and were constructed for teaching purposes; no real company data was used.

## The performance gap
- **Desired:** associates notice hazards in the course of normal work, rate how serious they are, and report them in a way a busy team lead can act on.
- **Actual (scenario):** staff can recite rules from slide-based onboarding, but hazards that have become familiar go unreported, and reports that do arrive are often vague.

## Causes, and which ones training can fix
| Cause | Training fix? | Better fix |
|---|---|---|
| Familiar hazards stop registering (normalization of risk) | Yes: practice noticing in a realistic space | Also: rotating walk-throughs by fresh eyes |
| Staff can't tell controlled conditions from hazards, so they either over-report or give up | Yes: distractors with explained feedback | — |
| Reports are vague and slow | Yes: practice a structured report | A short report form with the same four fields |
| A mirror nobody owns drifted out of alignment | **No** | Named owner and a weekly equipment walk |
| Put-away under time pressure leaves overhanging pallets | Partly | Load stops on beams (engineering) |

The "Is it training?" page in Module 4 walks learners through the same distinction.

## Constraints
- 45 minutes, at a training-station desktop or on a phone.
- Mixed literacy and English levels: plain language at about B1, icons with text, no timers.
- Must not imply legal compliance: "This course does not replace training required by law or by your employer."
- No injuries shown.

## What success would look like (not measured here)
More hazard reports with complete information, fewer near-misses tied to the same causes, and fewer reports of controlled conditions. None of this has been measured. No learners have used the course.
